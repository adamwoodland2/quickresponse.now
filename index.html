    What you can encode

    • Link QR code

      Opens a web page — a menu, a form, a video, your booking page.

    • Text QR code

      Shows a message, a serial number, a reference — anything up to a few thousand characters.

    • Wi‑Fi QR code

      Guests scan to join your network without typing the password. Works with iPhone and Android cameras.

    • Contact (vCard) QR code

      Saves your name, number, email and company to their phone in one tap — a business card that never runs out.

    • Email QR code

      Opens a new email to you with the subject and message already filled in.

    • SMS QR code

      Opens a text message to a number with the wording ready to send.

    • Phone QR code

      Offers to dial a number — for flyers, vans and shop windows.

    • Location QR code

      Opens a pin in the phone's maps app from a latitude and longitude.

    Why is this free?

    A QR code is just a picture — the squares are your link or text. There is no server in between, so nothing to host, nothing to expire, nothing to charge for. Sites whose "free" codes stop working after a week route them through their own redirect; that is how they switch them off. Codes made here point straight at your content.

    What happens to what I type?

    Nothing. The code is built on your device, in this page. What you type is never sent anywhere or stored — load the page, switch off your connection, and it still works. Only your style choices are remembered, in your own browser.

    Printing tips

    Phones scan from about ten times the code's width: a 2 cm code from 20 cm, a 30 cm code from across a room. Keep the clear border — it is part of the code. Use SVG for anything printed and PNG for screens and documents. Raise the damage tolerance for small, laminated or outdoor prints.
